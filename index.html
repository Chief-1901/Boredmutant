How it works.

Three phases. You can stop after any of them and keep what you have.

Phase 1

Audit.

2 weeks, fixed fee.

  1. Week 1We sit with the team doing the work. We list every tool, every seat, and the job each one really does.
  2. Week 2We time the work, pick what to replace first, and write a fixed-price spec with the payback.

You get the spec. It is yours to keep, even if you never build with us.

Phase 2

Replace.

3 to 6 weeks, fixed price.

  1. BuildOn your stack, connected to the systems you keep. Code goes to your repository from day one.
  2. TestOn your past cases, side by side with the old tools, until your team signs off.
  3. SwitchWe move the data, switch the system on, and cancel the seats it replaced.

You get a working system, the code, and a smaller software bill.

Phase 3

Run.

Monthly, flat fee.

  1. WatchWe monitor every run and fix what breaks, usually before you notice.
  2. ImproveSmall changes ship as your process changes. No change requests, no tickets.
  3. ReportEach month: what ran, what was fixed, and the numbers against week one.

You can stop any month. The system keeps running in your accounts.

Questions, answered.

Who maintains it after launch?

We do, on the monthly run plan. Or your own team does, with the runbook and the code we hand over. Either way it lives in your accounts.

Is it secure?

It runs in your cloud, under your security policies. Each connector gets only the access its job needs, and every action is logged with who approved it.

What happens if we stop working together?

Nothing switches off. You keep the code, the accounts, the data and a runbook. Another engineer can pick it up on day one.

How do you price?

A fixed fee for the audit, a fixed price for the build, quoted in the spec before you commit, then a flat monthly fee to run it. Never per seat.

What will you not replace?

Tools that do one job well for everyone: email, payroll, your accounting ledger. We replace the tools that make your team bend its process to fit.

Where does AI come in?

Two places. Agents that draft work for a person to approve, and AI-assisted engineering, which is why a build takes weeks instead of quarters.

Who is this for?

US companies of 50 to 500 people whose teams spend their days between too many tools.
