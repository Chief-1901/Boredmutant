Where placements stall

The offer is the easy part. The onboarding paperwork is where the week goes.

Unlike a tax deadline, your chase has no calendar. It is a rolling queue, one per candidate, each on its own clock. That is the kind of tracking humans are worst at.

Questions

What agency owners ask.

Is chasing I-9s automatically a compliance problem?

The system sends reminders and detects that a file arrived. It does not verify, complete, review or store I-9 documentation, and it makes no eligibility determination. Your existing process and your existing reviewers are unchanged; they get the paperwork sooner. We are not employment lawyers, and if your counsel wants to see exactly what sends and when, the dry-run output is a document you can hand them.

Does it work with our ATS?

No. Whatever spreadsheet your coordinator actually keeps is the input, and the sends go through your existing mailbox. Reading directly from the ATS is a later conversation, once the chaser has proved itself.

Can different recruiters have different signatures?

Yes. Each candidate's sequence sends under the recruiter who owns them, from that recruiter's mailbox, in their signature. To the candidate it looks exactly like the person they have been talking to all along, because it is.

There are cheaper onboarding document tools. Why not use one?

Often you should, and plenty of agencies run fine on one. The question is what the document is. A generic collection tool sends the candidate a link to upload into its platform, and prices by storage because it keeps the files. An I-9 comes with supporting identity documents, and a background check consent comes with personal data, so that arrangement puts a third party in custody of exactly the paperwork your compliance process exists to control. Nothing is stored here: the candidate replies to your recruiter's email and the attachment lands in your recruiter's inbox. It also changes the response rate. A candidate who has been talking to one recruiter for two weeks will reply to that person's email faster than they will create an account on a system they have never seen.

What about candidates who go completely dark?

After the final escalation, the item stops chasing and appears in your Friday digest as unresolved with the full contact history. That is the moment for a recruiter to make a phone call, which is what should have been happening on day eight anyway, rather than on day thirty when someone noticed.
