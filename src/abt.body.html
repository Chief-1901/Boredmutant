Why this exists

The work between the work.

Every professional services firm has a category of work that produces nothing and cannot be skipped. Asking a client for a bank statement. Asking again. Checking whether they sent it. Noting that they did. Telling the preparer they can start.

Nobody is bad at their job for spending time on it, because the work genuinely has to happen. But it is the least valuable hour in the building, and it lands disproportionately on the people whose time is worth the most, because they are the ones who notice it is not happening.

That is a solvable problem, and it is narrow enough to solve completely. Not "improved with AI", but actually removed, with a system that either has the document or does not, and behaves correctly in both cases. That narrowness is why BoredMutant sells one thing well instead of general automation vaguely.

How I work

Four commitments, all of them checkable.

These are not values on a wall. Each one is something you can hold me to, and notice immediately if I break.

  • Fixed scope and fixed fee, agreed in writing before any payment. No hourly billing, no discovery phase that quietly becomes the project. If it turns out to be bigger than we thought, that is my estimate that was wrong, not your invoice that goes up.
  • Nothing sends until you have read it. Every build runs two days in dry-run mode. You see every message the system would have sent, in writing, before a single real one goes out.
  • It runs on your accounts, not mine. Your mailbox, your infrastructure, your credentials, handed over encrypted. If I disappeared tomorrow, your chaser would keep running and another engineer could read it.
  • Month to month, always. No annual contract, no minimum term, no platform to migrate off. The retainer has to be worth paying every month or you should stop paying it.

What I turn down

Saying no to the wrong work is most of the job.

Not a fit

When chasing isn't your real bottleneck

Sometimes the call reveals that documents are not the problem. Capacity is, or scoping is, or one client who takes up half the firm. I would rather tell you that in fifteen minutes than sell you a chaser that solves nothing.

Not a fit

When it needs to be a platform

If what you actually want is a client portal with logins, dashboards and a product roadmap, that is a different company and a much larger budget. This is deliberately a small system that does one thing without asking your clients to learn anything.

Not a fit

When the wording can't be yours

The system sends only templates you have written and approved. If nobody at your firm has time to sign off on three paragraphs of email, this will not work, because that approval is the whole safety mechanism.

Not a fit

When you want the AI to improvise

People occasionally ask for a system that writes its own replies to clients. I will not build that for a firm whose reputation depends on precise communication. The value here is that it is boringly predictable.

The honest part

BoredMutant is new.

There is no client logo wall on this site, because there is nothing true to put on one yet. Every studio has a first client, and pretending otherwise with stock photography and invented testimonials is exactly the thing that makes small companies look untrustworthy.

So the terms are built to make going first rational rather than brave. The founding rate is genuinely lower, and it is capped at three firms per industry so it stays meaningful rather than becoming the price. In exchange, if the system works, I ask for a testimonial and one introduction, nothing else.

Beyond that, the risk is structurally low by design. The two-day dry run means you see the behaviour before any client does. Month-to-month means a bad month is a bad month, not a bad year. And because everything runs on your own mailbox and infrastructure, the worst realistic outcome is that you stop paying and go back to chasing manually, with a cleaner tracker than you started with.
