The daily run

One pass, every weekday morning.

Each open item is evaluated on its own. Watch it run. The pulse follows a single document from the list to the send.

Never writes

Every word a client reads was written and approved by your firm. The system chooses timing, not language.

Files stay closed

It detects that an attachment exists. It does not open, download, store or forward it.

Never over-sends

Once a document is received, that sequence is finished. There is no fourth email.

Only your list

It only contacts people on the list you handed over, about documents you listed.

What's actually built

The diagram is the part that takes an afternoon.

Workflow engines are a commodity, and the shape above is not where the work is. These are the parts that take a week and are specific to your firm.

Mailbox layer

Sending as you, safely

Authenticating against your Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace tenant with least-privilege access, threading replies correctly so conversations don't fragment, and making sure everything lands in your Sent folder rather than arriving from a stranger.

Reply handling

Telling a document from a question

An out-of-office is not a reply, and "sending tomorrow" is not a document. Getting this boundary right is what keeps the system from either nagging someone who already answered or going quiet on someone who didn't.

State

Knowing where every item stands

One record per client per document: stage, last contact, next due date, who paused it and why. This is what makes the Friday digest possible and what stops two staff members chasing the same person.

Your wording

Three templates that sound like your firm

Drafted with you, not for you. Firms that chase gently and firms that chase hard both work, but the escalation has to match how you actually speak to clients, or your staff will quietly turn it off.

Before anything real sends

Every new firm runs two full days in dry-run mode. The workflow executes exactly as above, but instead of sending, it writes each message it would have sent into a file for you to read. Nobody receives anything until you have looked at that file and said go.
